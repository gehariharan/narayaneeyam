## Findings

### F-D002-STYLE-01 — D002 series-style drift

**Severity:** medium · **Confidence:** high · **Outcome:** needs-fix on all ten D002 records. D002 candidates show more dimensional face, skin and cloth modelling, bright reflective-looking gold and polished depth/lighting than the matte mineral-pigment mural treatment specified for this series. This is a chapter-wide style issue, visible on both deities and human figures, not an anatomy defect. The independent D002 style review at `/home/gehariharan/OneDrive/Pictures/Narayaneeyam Studio/artifacts/d002/style-review-v001.md` also calls for correction; this audit freshly inspected all ten currently published versions.

**Repair direction:** Retain identities, scene content and valid iconography while flattening the treatment: firm drawn contours, matte mineral colour fields, shallow stylised space, patterned gold instead of reflective highlights, and restrained lighting across people and deities.

### F-D003-IDENTITY-01 — possible repeated Bhattathiri in D003 S001

**Severity:** low · **Confidence:** low · **Outcome:** uncertain. In the full-bleed gathering, the front seated listener (approximately x=.20–.41, y=.34–.98) and the storyteller (approximately x=.50–.72, y=.31–.98) both appear to share several Bhattathiri markers: dark tied-back hair, dark beard, vertical tilak, cream dhoti and maroon shoulder cloth. The scene plan asks for one storyteller and says Bhattathiri may appear among listeners. A close view confirmed the figures are separate bodies with attached limbs; the unresolved issue is whether the matching identity markers accidentally depict Bhattathiri twice. This is an uncertain identity observation, not a theological judgment or a confirmed defect.

**Repair direction if the figures are intended to be distinct:** Differentiate the storyteller’s face, hair, tilak or clothing from Bhattathiri. If both are meant to be Bhattathiri, retain only one appearance.

1. Bind every displayed image to a specific source file, version, full SHA-256, review role and deployed URL key before visual review.
2. Count expected, inspected, missing and alternate images; inspect every image individually at usable resolution and record any too-small or unavailable figure as uncertain.
3. Count visible arms, hands and feet per person; trace each limb to its shoulder/hip; inspect finger count, joints, proportions, attachment and object ownership. Use a close view whenever an overlap makes ownership unclear.
4. Read the stanza plan and relevant character contract before judging divine anatomy. Treat explicitly prescribed additional arms/heads as valid only when count, attachment and form match that scene’s instructions.
5. Compare faces and bodies within the same scene for accidental duplication, while distinguishing intentional symbolic sequences, crowd templates that are not visibly copied artifacts, and recurring identities across different stanzas.
6. Check for fused/overlapping people, floating attributes, malformed faces/eyes, clipped principal features, readable text, watermarks and material style drift.
7. Separate observed defects from uncertain identity or iconography judgments; assign stable IDs, severity, confidence, precise subject/location, visual outcome and a concise repair direction.
8. Keep audit disposition separate from prior publication/approval state; prior approval does not turn an observed defect into a pass.
